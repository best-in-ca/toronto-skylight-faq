Skylight Accessories Guide

Blinds, shades, smart controls, and integration — which accessories are worth it, which aren't, and when to add them.

Most homeowners install a skylight and then live with it as-is. But the accessory category — blinds, shades, smart controls — is what turns a passable skylight into a great one. A south-facing skylight without solar-control glazing or a shade is uncomfortable in summer; a high vented unit you can't reach to operate becomes decoration. Here's what's worth adding, and when.

Shades and blinds

Manual blackout shades

$200–400 per unit installed. Best for bedrooms and any room where light needs to be controllable. Manual is fine if the skylight is within reach (under 8 ft ceiling).

Manual light-filtering shades

$150–350. Softens harsh midday light without fully blocking it. Popular in living rooms and kitchens.

Motorized blackout shades

$400–800. The difference-maker. Best for:

  • High openings (vaulted ceilings, second-storey bathrooms)
  • Multiple skylights operated together
  • Smart-home integration with sunrise/sunset automation
  • Anyone who values "the room is comfortable" over "I had to climb a step ladder to close the blinds"

Insect screens (for vented units)

Standard accessory for any vented skylight — $80–150. Don't open a vented skylight in summer without one

Smart-home integration

Velux Active

The most mature ecosystem in the skylight market. Integrates with HomeKit (Apple), Google Home, and IFTTT. Features:

  • Automatic close on rain (sensor included)
  • Temperature-triggered ventilation (open when interior temp rises above set point)
  • Sunrise/sunset blind automation
  • Voice control via Siri, Google Assistant
  • Mobile app monitoring and manual control

See smart-home skylight systems for typical install scope. Add-on cost: $400–900 for the controller plus motorized units to control.

Other systems

Fakro has its own electric vented systems with remote operation but doesn't compete with Velux Active for ecosystem depth. Third-party Z-Wave or Zigbee controllers can sometimes retrofit older motorized units, but compatibility is hit-or-miss.

Operators and remotes

Pole operator (manual)

$30–60. The simplest accessory — a telescoping pole to open and close a high-mounted manual vented skylight. Often included with the unit.

Wall-mounted electric switch

$120–250. For electric vented units. Better than remote alone — guests know how to use it, no battery to die.

Multi-channel remote

$150–300. Controls multiple skylights from one remote. Worth it if you have 3+ units.

Solar accessories

Solar panel for vented unit

Built into solar-vented units; no separate cost. Trickle-charges a small battery that runs the operator. Reduces wiring requirements at install — major selling point for retrofit projects.

Battery backup

$80–150. For Velux Active automation — keeps the rain sensor and auto-close working during power outages.

Light kits and fixtures

Skylight perimeter LED

$200–500 installed. LED strip around the inside of the shaft. Adds artificial illumination at night when the skylight is just a dark window in the ceiling. Especially valuable in stairwells and hallways.

Wall washers paired with skylight

$300–800 installed. Recessed lights that wash a feature wall — turning the room into a layered light environment by day or night. Premium feature but transformative in living spaces.

Sun control add-ons

Awning (exterior)

$300–600 per skylight. External awning that blocks solar heat gain before it hits the glass. Most effective heat-control accessory.

Reflective films (interior)

$60–150. Aftermarket sun-control films. Cheaper than awnings but less effective. Good retrofit for older units without solar-control glazing.

Sound and security

Acoustic upgrades

For skylights near busy roads or under flight paths, laminated glass with acoustic interlayer adds significant sound reduction. Adds $200–400 to the unit cost. Best to spec at purchase, not retrofit.

Skylight security bars or grilles

For ground-floor or low-roof commercial installations. $150–400 per unit. See commercial skylight options.

The "add at install" vs "retrofit" question

Some accessories are easier to add at install:

  • Motorized blinds — much easier to install when ceiling is open. Retrofitting requires opening drywall
  • Electrical for vented units — easy to run wire during install, expensive after
  • Smart-home wiring — same logic
  • LED perimeter strip — needs to be wired into ceiling, easier at install

Others can be retrofit anytime:

  • Manual blinds and shades
  • Reflective films
  • External awnings
  • Solar-vented system (powers itself)

What to ask the installer

  • Which accessories do you recommend for my specific room?
  • What's harder to add later vs add now?
  • Can you wire for motorized blinds even if I want manual to start?
  • Do you stock Velux and Fakro accessories?
  • What's the warranty on the accessories vs the skylight?
