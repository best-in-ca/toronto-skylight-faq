Skylight Installation — What Actually Happens

Day-by-day breakdown of a skylight installation in a typical Toronto home, from quote to final inspection.

Most homeowners have never seen a skylight installation up close. The process is faster than people expect (1–2 days for most residential) but involves more steps than people realize. This guide walks through exactly what happens, so you know what to expect when the crew arrives.

Pre-install (week 0 — before crew arrives)

  • Site visit and measurement — confirm location, opening size, roof type
  • Quote and contract signed
  • Permits applied for if new opening (see new skylight installation services — includes permit handling)
  • Unit ordered — most major brands stock in Toronto-area warehouses; lead time 1–3 weeks
  • Flashing kit specified and ordered with the unit
  • Crew scheduled — typically 3–6 weeks out from contract signing

Day 1 morning — site prep and exterior cut

  • 7:30–8:30 AM — crew arrives, lays out tools, sets up interior protection (drop cloths, plastic sheeting around the work zone)
  • 8:30 AM — homeowner walkthrough with crew lead, confirming exact location and any last details
  • 9:00 AM — crew moves to roof; locates the work area from above
  • 9:30 AM — interior ceiling opened with a small pilot hole to confirm rafter location
  • 10:00 AM — exterior cut begins. Shingles in the work zone removed; opening framed in the roof deck
  • 11:30 AM — opening fully framed. Rough opening squared and ready for the unit

Day 1 afternoon — installation and flashing

  • 12:00 PM — skylight unit lifted to the roof; positioned in the opening
  • 12:30 PM — unit fastened to the rafters per manufacturer spec
  • 1:00 PM — ice-and-water shield installed around the curb (required by Ontario Building Code in eave zone, best practice everywhere)
  • 2:00 PM — manufacturer flashing kit installed. The most important part of the install — proper step flashing at the sides, head flashing at the top, apron at the bottom. See flashing kit options
  • 3:00 PM — shingles replaced over the flashing, with proper interweaving
  • 3:30 PM — exterior sealant applied at all critical seams
  • 4:00 PM — exterior work complete and watertight

Day 2 — interior finishing

  • 8:00 AM — crew returns; interior framing of the shaft
  • 9:00 AM — insulation installed around the shaft (R-30 minimum recommended for Toronto)
  • 10:00 AM — drywall installed on the interior shaft
  • 11:00 AM — taping and mudding begins. First coat applied
  • 1:00 PM — second coat of joint compound
  • 2:00 PM — sanding and final smoothing
  • 3:00 PM — primer applied
  • 3:30 PM — first paint coat (if same colour as ceiling, otherwise on day 3)
  • 4:30 PM — site cleanup, removal of protection, walkthrough with homeowner

Day 3 (if needed) — second paint coat and final

Many projects can finish in 2 days. Some require a third day for:

  • Custom-colour second paint coat that needs the first to fully dry
  • Splayed (flared) shaft work that takes longer to finish
  • Electrical work for vented unit or motorized blinds
  • Multiple-unit installations

Replacements run faster

A skylight replacement in an existing opening typically completes in 1 day:

  • Old unit removed (30–60 minutes)
  • Opening inspected, any rotted curb wood replaced
  • New flashing kit installed
  • New unit installed and secured
  • Shingles re-bedded over flashing
  • Interior touch-up of paint and drywall

Flat-roof installs are different

For flat-roof skylight installs, the sequence changes:

  • Day 1 — curb construction and waterproofing membrane work
  • Day 2 — unit installation and final membrane termination
  • Day 3 — interior framing and drywall

Flat-roof installs cost more ($2,200–4,800) primarily because of the curb construction time and the membrane work.

What inspections happen

For permitted new installations, two inspections are typical:

  • Rough-in inspection — after framing and flashing, before drywall. City inspector verifies opening size, framing integrity, and flashing
  • Final inspection — after interior finish work complete. Verifies code compliance and quality

For direct replacements in existing openings, no inspections typically required.

What you should do during the work

  • Be available the morning of day 1 for the walkthrough — 15 minutes
  • Keep pets contained, especially during interior drywall sanding (dust)
  • Stay flexible on noise — sawing, hammering, drilling for the first day
  • Have the parking arrangement set for the crew's truck
  • Check progress at end of day 1 — anything you notice gets fixed faster than after the project ends

Post-installation

  • Final walkthrough — verify all work, sign off
  • Receive product warranty information (Velux 10 years on glass, 20 on frame; similar for Fakro)
  • Receive installer workmanship warranty (typically 5–10 years on flashing and labour from a reputable installer like Toronto Skylight Installers)
  • Save the paint code and any special instructions for future touch-ups
